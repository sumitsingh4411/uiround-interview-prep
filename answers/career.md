# Behavioral and career interview questions and answers

The questions interviewers open with, each with a short answer you can say out loud. Every question links to the full answer on [UIRound](https://uiround.com), with examples, comparisons and the follow-up questions that come next. Free, no account needed.

## Beginner

### How do you answer "What are your strengths and weaknesses?" as a frontend engineer

For strengths, name one or two specific to frontend work, such as turning vague designs into accessible components or debugging performance problems, and prove each with a short story and a result. For weaknesses, name a real one that does not disqualify you for the role, show you understand its cost, and explain the concrete habit you use to manage it. Self-awareness matters more than the weakness itself.

[Read the full answer](https://uiround.com/answers/how-do-you-answer-what-are-your-strengths-and-weaknesses)

### How do you answer "Why do you want to work here?"

Answer "Why do you want to work here?" with three specific points: something about the company's product or engineering that genuinely interests you, evidence from your own work that you are suited to it, and what you want to learn or contribute there. A good answer could not be said about any other company. Generic praise ("great culture", "industry leader") tells the interviewer you did no research.

[Read the full answer](https://uiround.com/answers/how-do-you-answer-why-do-you-want-to-work-here)

### How do you answer "Why frontend engineering?"

Answer "Why frontend engineering?" by naming what genuinely draws you to the work, such as building the part of the product people actually touch, the fast feedback loop, or hard problems like performance, accessibility and state, then prove it with one short story from your own experience. Show that you understand frontend as serious engineering, not "making things pretty", and say where you want to grow within it.

[Read the full answer](https://uiround.com/answers/how-do-you-answer-why-frontend-engineering)

### How should a frontend engineer answer "Tell me about yourself"?

Answer "Tell me about yourself" in 60 to 90 seconds with three parts: what you do now and the kind of frontend work you are strongest at, one project with a concrete, measured result, and why this role is the logical next step. It is not your life story or a recital of your resume. It is a trailer that tells the interviewer what to ask about next.

[Read the full answer](https://uiround.com/answers/how-should-a-frontend-engineer-answer-tell-me-about-yourself)

### What questions should you ask at the end of a frontend interview?

Ask two or three specific questions that help you decide whether you want the job and show how you think as a frontend engineer: how the team ships and keeps quality, what the hardest frontend problems are right now, and what success looks like in the first six months. Tailor them to the interviewer's role. Never say "No, I think you covered everything"; it reads as lack of interest.

[Read the full answer](https://uiround.com/answers/what-questions-should-you-ask-at-the-end-of-a-frontend-interview)

## Intermediate

### How do you answer "Tell me about a disagreement with a teammate"?

Pick a real, work-related disagreement where both sides had reasonable views, such as a choice of state management or how much to test. Tell it with STAR: the situation, what was at stake, how you listened, gathered data or ran a small experiment, how the decision was made, and the result. End with what you learned. Interviewers care less about who was right than about how you handled it.
